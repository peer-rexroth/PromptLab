Getting started

What PromptLab does
PromptLab keeps your reusable AI prompts, each with variables: placeholders you fill in or pick from before copying. Write a prompt once, then change the tone, language or audience with a click instead of retyping it.
Your first prompt
New prompt: click the + beside the search field, or press Cmd/Ctrl+N. It opens with a short example and its name field ready to type in. A new prompt takes the category you are currently filtering by.
Write the template: type your prompt in the Template box and put a placeholder wherever the text should change: {{Tone}}, [[Checks]] or ((Topic)). Then click Sync (the circular-arrows button in the header) so PromptLab creates a control for each new placeholder.
Preview shows the finished text with your current choices filled in, so you see exactly what will be copied. Text and checklist variables you haven't filled in show as (empty).
Copy prompt: the blue button copies the finished text to your clipboard (the Preview's own Copy button does the same). Cmd/Ctrl+Enter works from anywhere.
Finding your way around
Click a prompt in the sidebar to open it. ↑ ↓ switch to the previous or next prompt straight away, even while the search field has focus; the ends stop rather than wrap.
Locked prompts: the prompts that come with the app are locked (read-only) so you can use them without changing them by accident. You can still pick options, type into text fields and apply presets. Click the padlock in the header to unlock one for editing. Clone makes a separate copy you can change without touching the original; it keeps the lock, so unlock the copy first.
The header has a category pill, then one group of icons (Favorite, Lock, Sync, Clone | Delete, each with a tooltip), then Copy prompt.
Three kinds of variable
{{name}} Option variable: pick one value. After Sync, add the choices (for example formal, casual) with + Option; the selected pill is what gets inserted.
[[name]] Instruction variable: a checklist. Tick any number of items; each ticked item is inserted as a bullet.
((name)) Text variable: type any value straight into the field in the variables panel.
Sync after editing the template. It reads the template, creates entries for new placeholders and drops entries whose placeholder is gone. Existing variables keep their options and selections. To show a placeholder as literal text, put a backslash in front of it: \{{name}} is copied as {{name}}.
Managing options
Click + Option (or + Instruction) next to a variable to add a choice.
Rename: double-click an option pill to edit it in place; on a checklist, use the pencil that appears when you hover a row.
Reorder: drag option pills, or checklist rows, to rearrange them.
Remove an option: hover its pill and click the small ✕ inside it (on a checklist, the ✕ at the end of the row). A variable always keeps at least one option. The ✕ at the far right of a variable's row removes the whole variable; if its placeholder is still in the template, Sync will add it back, so delete the placeholder from the template as well.
Saving and applying
A preset remembers a combination of choices so you can switch to it in one click. Pick your options, click Save preset and name it. Click a preset chip to apply it; the chip of the preset that matches your current choices is highlighted.
Text variables are not part of a preset. A preset only switches option and instruction variables, so whatever you typed into a ((text)) field, such as a name or a topic, stays put while you step through presets.
Clear is built in and can't be renamed or deleted. It resets every variable, text fields included, and is highlighted only when everything is empty.
Managing presets
Rename: double-click a preset's name, then Enter to save or Esc to cancel. Names must be unique within a prompt.
Delete with the ✕ on the chip, and reorder by dragging chips.
On a locked prompt you can apply presets but not save, rename, reorder or delete them. Saving needs at least one option or instruction variable.
The sidebar list
All Prompts and Favorites sit at the top, then your Categories, each with a colour dot and a count. Click a row to filter the prompt list; click the selected category again to clear the filter. Empty categories are dimmed.
Click the Categories heading to collapse or expand the section. The choice is remembered on this device, and a collapsed section still shows the category you are filtering by.
Search matches prompt names and the text inside templates. Cmd/Ctrl+K jumps to the search field.
Categories and favorites
Set a prompt's category with the pill in its header; it opens a menu of your categories with a tick on the current one.
Manage categories with the sliders button on the Categories heading: add one (and pick its colour), rename it in place, click its colour dot to change it, drag to reorder, or delete it. A category can only be deleted when no prompt uses it.
Favorites: click the star in the sidebar row or in the header's icon group. Favorites float to the top of the list and have their own filter.
Undo and redo
In the template: Cmd/Ctrl+Z undoes and Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes, or use the two buttons above the template. Each prompt has its own history.
After a delete: deleting a prompt or category asks first, then shows a toast with Undo for a few seconds. Cmd/Ctrl+Z does the same when you are not typing in a field. Undo puts it back in the same place.
Version history
Each Sync saves a snapshot of the template, its variables and presets as they were before the edit. The Version history panel under the preview keeps the last three and shows what changed. Restore rolls back to one (your current state is saved first, so a restore can be undone), and the bin button clears the history.
Names, copies and deleting
Clone duplicates a prompt, with its variables, presets and lock state, as a copy named “… (copy)” that is not marked as a favorite. The copy opens straight away.
Rename by editing the title. Two prompts can't share a name, so a name that is already taken isn't accepted while you type.
The Appearance button
Text size: the small and large “A” in the aA menu switch between Medium and Large for the whole app. The size is saved with your library, so a linked file carries it to other computers.
Theme: Light, Dark, or Auto to follow your system's setting as it changes. The theme is a per-device choice, so each computer keeps its own.
The sidebar
The button at the left of the top bar, or Cmd/Ctrl+B, hides and shows the sidebar to give the editor more room. In a narrow window it starts hidden and floats over the editor when opened; choosing a prompt closes it again without changing your saved preference.
Where your prompts live
Your library is stored in this browser on this computer. It stays between sessions but is not synced to other devices, and clearing the browser's site data deletes it. There is no server and no account.
Link file (top bar) keeps a copy in a .json file you choose and updates it on every change, so it can live in a synced folder or be backed up. The chip then reads Linked (click it to unlink) and turns red as Reconnect if the browser drops permission. Linking needs Chrome or Edge.
The Data menu
Export downloads your whole library (prompts and categories) as a .json file.
Import loads a .json file. Merge adds what's new and keeps the newer copy of anything that exists in both; Replace swaps your library for the file's, and downloads a backup of what you had first.
Backups: PromptLab takes one automatic snapshot a day and keeps the last seven. Restore one from the Data menu; your current library is downloaded as a safety copy before it is replaced.
The bundled prompts
A new, empty library starts with a few ready-made prompts (locked), such as issue trees, meeting minutes and translation. They are only added to an empty library, never to one you already have.
